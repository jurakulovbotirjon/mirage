# Animal Kingdom: Vertebrates and Invertebrates

**Purpose:** Mirage knowledge-ingestion test fixture  
**Word count:** 300 (body only)  
**Type:** Synthetic factual test text; not an external source document

Animals belong to the kingdom Animalia. They can be grouped as vertebrates or invertebrates, based on whether they have a backbone. Vertebrates possess a backbone and an internal skeleton. Invertebrates lack a backbone, although some have external skeletons or other supporting structures. Vertebrates include five major groups: fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds, and mammals. Fish are aquatic vertebrates that generally breathe through gills and use fins for movement. Most fish are ectothermic, meaning their body temperature changes with their surroundings. Amphibians include frogs, toads, and salamanders. Many begin life in water, breathe through gills as larvae, and later develop lungs or use their skin for gas exchange. Reptiles include snakes, lizards, turtles, crocodilians, and tuatara. They breathe with lungs and have skin covered by scales or protective plates. Most reptiles are ectothermic and lay eggs, although some species give birth to live young. Birds have feathers, beaks, and wings. They breathe with lungs, are endothermic, and lay eggs with hard shells. Mammals have hair or fur, and females produce milk to feed their young. Most mammals give birth to live young, although monotremes, such as the platypus, lay eggs. Invertebrates make up most described animal species. Their groups include insects, arachnids, crustaceans, mollusks, annelid worms, cnidarians, and echinoderms. Insects have six legs, three main body regions, and usually wings. Spiders and scorpions are arachnids; adults have eight legs and no antennae. Crustaceans include crabs, lobsters, and shrimp, and many have hard external skeletons. Mollusks include snails, clams, squid, and octopuses. Annelid worms, such as earthworms, have segmented bodies. Cnidarians include jellyfish, sea anemones, and corals; many have stinging cells. Echinoderms include sea stars and sea urchins and live in marine environments. Vertebrates and invertebrates occupy terrestrial, freshwater, and marine habitats. They perform ecological roles including grazing, predation, decomposition, filtration, and pollination.
